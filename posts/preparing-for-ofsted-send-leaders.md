Ofsted's renewed inspection framework, introduced in November 2025, replaced single-word judgements with report cards. Each evaluation area is graded on a five-point scale, and inclusion is now its own evaluation area as well as running through the others. For SEND leaders, that makes it more important than ever to know your provision well and to show its impact clearly.

## Know your SEND cohort

Inspectors will expect leaders to know who their pupils with SEND are, what their needs are and how well they are doing. Be ready to talk about:

- the number of pupils on SEN support and with EHCPs, and how this has changed;
- the main areas of need and how provision is designed around them;
- how pupils with SEND are identified, including any who are not yet on the register.

## Show impact, not just activity

A list of interventions isn't evidence of impact. Be ready to show how pupils with SEND are progressing from their starting points — including small steps for pupils with the most complex needs — and how you know the support is working. Link provision to outcomes, and be honest about what you've changed when something didn't work.

## Look at attendance, behaviour and suspensions

Compare attendance, suspensions and behaviour incidents for pupils with SEND with their peers. If gaps exist, know why, and what you're doing about them. Part-time timetables should be rare, short-term and reviewed.

## Curriculum and teaching

Inspectors will look at how well the curriculum and teaching meet the needs of pupils with SEND in lessons, not only in separate interventions. Be clear about the adaptations staff make and how you support and check them.

## Listen to pupils and families

Pupil voice and the views of parents and carers matter. Know what they think about the school's support, and what you've done in response.

## Make your self-evaluation match reality

Your self-evaluation should reflect what inspectors will see. Use monitoring evidence from across the year — observations, book looks, learning walks and pupil and parent views — rather than a single snapshot.

## A worked example

Before a recent inspection, the SEND leader at one secondary school prepared a short evidence summary rather than a large file. It covered the school's SEND cohort and how it had changed over three years; the main areas of need and the provision mapped to each; progress for pupils with SEND from their starting points, including small-step progress for pupils with the most complex needs; and attendance and suspension rates for pupils with SEND compared with their peers, with the actions taken to close the gaps.

She also chose three pupils whose stories showed the school's approach — one on SEN support, one with an EHCP in mainstream classes and one with complex needs in the resourced provision — and made sure their plans, reviews and evidence were up to date. During the inspection, inspectors spoke to two of these pupils and their teachers. Because the evidence was already gathered in one place, the conversation focused on the quality of support rather than finding documents.

## Common pitfalls

- **Describing activity instead of impact.** "We run 14 interventions" says little without evidence that they work.
- **Data without explanation.** If attendance for pupils with SEND is lower, know why and what you're doing.
- **A self-evaluation that's out of date.** It should reflect what's happening now, not last year.
- **Leaving teachers out.** Inspectors will talk to class teachers about how they adapt lessons; make sure they feel confident to explain it.

## Questions to discuss with your team

- Could every teacher explain how they adapt teaching for the pupils with SEND in their class?
- Where are the gaps between pupils with SEND and their peers, and what's our plan?
- Is our monitoring evidence organised by the inspection framework's evaluation areas?
- What do pupils and families tell us about our SEND provision?

## Quick checklist

- You can describe your SEND cohort, its needs and how it has changed.
- Progress from starting points is evidenced, including small steps.
- Attendance and suspensions for pupils with SEND are compared with peers.
- Adaptations in lessons are clear and monitored.
- Pupil and parent views are gathered and acted on.
- Self-evaluation is based on evidence from across the year.
