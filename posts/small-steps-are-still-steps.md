For many pupils with SEND, the milestones that dominate school data — a new reading level, a test score, a national curriculum stage — can stay still for months. That doesn't mean nothing is happening. It usually means the measure is too coarse to see the progress being made.

## Why big milestones hide progress

A pupil who moves from tolerating a group activity for one minute to joining in for ten has made significant progress. So has a pupil who begins to use a symbol to request a break rather than leaving the room. Neither will show up on a termly attainment grid. If the only record is "working at" a broad stage, staff, families and the pupil can all lose sight of how far they've come.

## Break outcomes into steps you can see

Start with the outcome that matters — often from the EHCP or support plan — and work backwards:

- **What would the pupil be doing when this is achieved?** Describe it in observable terms.
- **What is the smallest change you'd notice first?** That becomes the first step.
- **What sits in between?** Aim for steps that are achievable within a few weeks.

Steps should describe what the pupil does, in which context and with how much support. "Uses a now-and-next board with an adult prompt" and "uses a now-and-next board independently" are different steps, and both are worth recording.

## Recognise breadth as well as height

Progress isn't only upwards. A skill used in one room, with one adult, is a step; using it in different places, with different people, or for longer, is further progress. This is central to the Engagement Model, which asks schools to consider exploration, realisation, anticipation, persistence and initiation for pupils not yet engaged in subject-specific study.

## Evidence that tells the story

A short note, photo or video at the moment a step is secured is far more powerful than a tick at the end of term. Good evidence:

- shows what the pupil did, not only that a target was met;
- notes the context and level of support;
- is easy to share with parents, who often see different progress at home.

## Celebrate it

Make small steps visible. Share them in reviews, with families and with the pupil in a way that works for them. When the whole team can see momentum, it becomes easier to keep going — and to spot quickly when a step isn't coming and the plan needs to change.

## A worked example

Amira, in Year 9, has profound and multiple learning difficulties. One of her EHCP outcomes is to communicate her choices. Her termly data showed "no change" for two terms, which worried her parents.

Her team broke the outcome into steps: looking towards a preferred object when two are offered; reaching towards it; reaching consistently across different sessions; making a choice with a familiar adult and then with a less familiar one; and using a switch to choose between two options. Short videos captured each step as it happened. At her review, the team could show that Amira had secured four new steps since the start of the year, including choosing with a new member of staff. Her parents said it was the first time they had been able to see her progress so clearly.

## Common pitfalls

- **Steps that are too big.** If a step takes a whole term, break it down further.
- **Steps that describe the adult, not the pupil.** "Will be supported to…" doesn't tell you what the pupil can now do.
- **Evidence without context.** A photo with no note about support or setting is hard to interpret later.
- **Forgetting generalisation.** A skill used only with one adult isn't fully secure.

## Questions to discuss with your team

- Which pupils' progress is hidden by our current measures?
- Are our steps small enough to show change within a few weeks?
- How do we record the level of support and the setting?
- How do we share small-step progress with families between reviews?

## Quick checklist

- Each outcome is broken into observable steps achievable within weeks.
- Steps describe context and level of support.
- Breadth (new people, places, times) is recognised as progress.
- Evidence is captured when the step happens, not at the end of term.
- Progress is shared with families and the pupil.
